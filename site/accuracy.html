How accurate is Stickler?

Good enough to learn a song from, not perfect. Here is how it works, how it was measured and every number, in plain words.

How it works

Two open models listen to the song. ADTOF hears each hit and which drum it is. Beat This! finds the beat, the bars and the first beat of each bar. Stickler puts the hits on the beat grid, writes equal bars once with repeat signs, and sets the sheet music with Verovio. All of this runs on your computer in about 15 seconds for a 4-minute song. Demucs only runs when you save the drum track on its own.

How it was measured

Stickler's notes were compared hit by hit with sheet music written by people. A hit counts as found when the same drum is played within 50 milliseconds of the reference. From that comes the F1 score: 1 means every hit found and nothing invented, 0 means nothing right. The score is averaged over five drums: kick, snare, toms, hi-hat and cymbals (macro-F1).

  • 25 own songs: commercial rock, metal and pop recordings, compared with transcriptions from musescore.com. This is the fairest test, as no model was trained on them.
  • MDB Drums: 23 tracks from a research data set.
  • ENST-Drums: drummer 3, the part of this data set no model was trained on.

The people's sheet music has mistakes too. 100 spots where Stickler and the reference disagreed were judged by ear, without knowing which was which: about half were mistakes of the reference. Taken out, the score of the own songs rises.

The numbers

  • Fast gets about 87 of 100 hits right on real music, once the mistakes in the people's sheet music are taken out (between 84 and 90 with 95 % certainty).
  • Quality separates the drums first and uses a second model. Against the references it is slightly better, after correcting them Fast is ahead, and it takes about 56 times as long.
  • Where it slips: fast fills and quiet ghost notes. A wrong tempo or first beat is fixed in the app with one click.
  • What did not help: newer models, combining several models, and tuning thresholds looked good on research data but not on rock and metal.
