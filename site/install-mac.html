Installing Stickler on a Mac

In short

You need a Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 26. Download Stickler, drag it to Applications, and allow it once in System Settings. It takes about two minutes.

macOS asks for that one extra step because Stickler does not come from the App Store. Apple charges developers a yearly fee to skip it; Stickler is free and does without.

Step by step

  1. Download it

    Choose Download for Mac. The file, a disk image ending in .dmg, lands in your Downloads folder.

  2. Drag Stickler to Applications

    Open the downloaded file. A window shows Stickler next to the Applications folder: drag Stickler onto it. Then eject the disk image in the Finder’s sidebar.

  3. Open it once

    Open Stickler from Applications. macOS says it could not check the app and does not open it. Choose Done; nothing is wrong.

  4. Allow it in System Settings

    Open System Settings › Privacy & Security and scroll down to Security. Next to the note that Stickler was blocked, choose Open Anyway. The button is there for about an hour after you tried to open Stickler.

    Apple shows these windows in its own guide: Open a Mac app from an unknown developer.

  5. Confirm

    macOS asks once more: choose Open Anyway and confirm with your password or Touch ID. Stickler opens. From now on it opens like any other app.

The first start

Stickler needs trained models to hear the drums. It shows which ones, their licences and their size, and downloads them only after you agree. After that it works without the internet; your songs stay on your Mac.

No “Open Anyway” button?

Try to open Stickler again and look in System Settings within the hour. If the button still does not show, open the Terminal app, paste this line, press Return, and open Stickler again:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Stickler.app

The line removes the mark macOS puts on everything downloaded with a browser; it changes nothing else.

Removing it again

Choose Uninstall Stickler… in the Stickler menu: Stickler removes its models and moves itself to the Bin; a box removes your settings, recent songs and corrections too. Without the app: drag Stickler from Applications to the Bin.
